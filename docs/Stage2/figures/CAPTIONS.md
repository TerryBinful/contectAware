# Figure captions

For *Does the Handover Margin Earn Its Place? A Factorial Comparison of Temporal Decision
Policies for Context-Based Continuous Smartphone Authentication*.

Every figure is generated by the script of the same name in this directory, reading only
CSV and JSON files under
`experiment_Files/Stage2/results/mechanism_comparison_v2/analysis/`. Nothing under
`analysis/` was modified. Each script prints the numbers it drew and writes them to `checks/<stem>_checks.json`;
`CHECKS.md` collects them. Rebuild everything with `./build.sh`.

Throughout, the cell class with both a margin and a dwell is called **"margin + dwell"**,
never "hysteresis". In 3GPP Event A3, *hysteresis* is the margin term alone and
*time-to-trigger* is a separate dwell term, so naming the composed cell "hysteresis"
would misstate the analogue the paper is testing.

## Figure 1 — Factorial response surface at target FAR 0.05

**Files:** `rendered/fig1_factorial_surface.{png,pdf}` · **Script:** `scripts/fig1_factorial_surface.py`
**Source:** `analysis/factorial/response_surface.csv`, rows with `target == 0.05`

> **Figure 1. The factorial response surface at a matched operating point of FAR 0.05.**
> Margin *m* ∈ {0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2} in rows, dwell *k* ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5, 10} in columns. Top:
> excess transitions per 180-frame test sequence, counted above the minimum of two that a
> correct genuine–impostor–genuine sequence requires. Bottom: false rejection rate over
> genuine frames. Each cell gives the mean across the participants for whom that cell had
> a feasible calibration operating point, with that participant count below it. Heavy
> outlines mark the four cell classes formed by the two factor levels: instantaneous
> (*m* = 0, *k* = 1), dwell only (*m* = 0, *k* > 1), margin only (*m* > 0, *k* = 1) and
> margin + dwell (*m* > 0, *k* > 1). Three of the twenty cells had no feasible operating
> point for any participant and are greyed.
>
> **The cells are not paired and must not be compared as if they were.** The feasible
> participant set differs from cell to cell, from 6 participants to 31, and the cells with
> the most aggressive settings are supported by the fewest participants — the apparent
> "0.000 excess transitions" at *m* = 0.05, *k* = 10 rests on 9 participants, and pairs
> with a false rejection rate of 0.588. Read the broad shape only: stability improves down
> and to the right, and lockout worsens along the same direction. The preregistered
> participant-paired tests, not this surface, are what the claims rest on.

## Figure 2 — The stability–lockout plane

**Files:** `rendered/fig2_stability_lockout.{png,pdf}` · **Script:** `scripts/fig2_stability_lockout.py`
**Sources:** `analysis/families/mechanism_metrics.csv`,
`analysis/secondary/far_0.03/mechanism_metrics.csv`,
`analysis/secondary/far_0.07/mechanism_metrics.csv`

> **Figure 2. Every decision mechanism trades lockout for stability, and none escapes the
> trade.** Each filled marker is one mechanism at the primary calibration target of
> FAR 0.05, positioned by its mean false rejection rate (horizontal) and mean excess
> transitions per sequence (vertical), with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals on both
> axes. Lower left is better. The small open markers give the same mechanism calibrated to
> FAR 0.03 and FAR 0.07 and are joined to it by a thin trail, so a position that is an
> artefact of the chosen operating point shows up as a long trail. Margin + dwell is drawn
> in the accent colour as the mechanism under test. Note the broken vertical axis: the
> instantaneous rule sits an order of magnitude above every other mechanism, and on one
> continuous scale the remaining nine collapse into an unreadable band. Both segments are
> linear with the same tick spacing; only the empty interval from 0.95 to 1.55 excess
> transitions is removed, and no marker or interval falls inside it.
>
> **The mechanisms are not paired with one another.** Each is summarised over the
> participants for whom it had a feasible operating point at that target, and those sets
> differ — from 18 participants (margin, dual threshold, at FAR 0.03) to 31. The
> differences between mechanisms here are descriptive; the paired tests are reported
> separately.

## Figure 3 — Concentration of instability

**Files:** `rendered/fig3_instability_concentration.{png,pdf}` · **Script:** `scripts/fig3_instability_concentration.py`
**Source:** `analysis/families/sequence_metrics.csv`, rows with `target == 0.05` and
`mechanism == 'instantaneous'`

> **Figure 3. The instability the decision layer exists to fix is concentrated in a few
> participants.** Cumulative share of excess transitions against participants ranked from
> most to least unstable, for the instantaneous rule at target FAR 0.05; each participant
> contributes the mean over their own test sequences. The dashed diagonal is the
> counterfactual in which every participant contributes equally. The five most unstable
> participants of 30 account for 65.6% of all excess transitions, while 10 of 30 never
> flip spuriously at all and 124 of the 173 test sequences contain zero excess
> transitions.
>
> This is a within-mechanism decomposition over a fully paired set: all 30 participants
> and all 173 sequences enter, and the curve is a redistribution of one total rather than
> a comparison between conditions. It is the reason a mean over participants understates
> what a temporal decision policy does for the participants who need one, and the reason
> the paired tests in Section 5 have so little to detect — most participants have no
> instability to remove.

## Figure 4 — Score validity under the context shortcut, F3 versus F7

**Files:** `rendered/fig4_score_validity_f3_f7.{png,pdf}` · **Script:** `scripts/fig4_score_validity_f3_f7.py`
**Sources:** `analysis/secondary/score_validity_f3_f7.csv`,
`analysis/secondary/score_validity_summary.json`

> **Figure 4. Removing the location and device-state features removes most of the score's
> discriminative power.** One line per participant, from the F3 feature set, which retains
> location and device-state features, to F7, which removes them. Mean AUC falls from 0.990
> to 0.758; the mean paired difference is 0.232, with a 95% bootstrap confidence interval
> of [0.191, 0.273]. The dashed rule marks chance. One participant falls below chance
> under F7 and is drawn in the accent colour.
>
> **This comparison is fully paired**: all 31 participants have both an F3 and an F7 AUC,
> and the interval is over the per-participant differences. AUC falls for 30 participants
> and is unchanged for one; it rises for none. No decision layer is run on F7 — this is a
> descriptive construct-validity probe (Section 4.4), and it bounds how much of the
> apparent authentication performance in this paper is context recognition rather than
> user recognition.

## Figure 5 (optional) — One sequence under three policies

**Files:** `rendered/fig5_sequence_schematic.{png,pdf}` · **Script:** `scripts/fig5_sequence_schematic.py`
**Sources:** `f3/scores/7CE37510.parquet`, `analysis/factorial/operating_points.csv`

> **Figure 5. What a margin and a dwell each do to one real score stream.** Test sequence
> `7CE37510_tsts1`: 60 genuine frames, then 60 impostor frames (shaded), then 60 genuine
> frames, at roughly one frame per minute. Top: the ECDF-normalised score, with the
> instantaneous threshold θ dashed and the accept and reject thresholds θ ± *m*/2 of the
> margin + dwell policy bounding the shaded dead band. Below: the authentication state
> under the instantaneous rule, under dwell *k* = 2, and under margin *m* = 0.05 with
> dwell *k* = 2; two transitions is the minimum a correct sequence requires. Each policy
> uses that participant's own θ from the FAR 0.05 calibration, so the three are compared
> at a matched operating point. The states are produced by the frozen rule module
> `src/decision_v2.py` itself, not reimplemented for the figure. Inset: the 3GPP Event A3
> analogue, in which a dead band of width 2·Hys must be crossed and the condition must
> then hold for a time-to-trigger interval before a handover fires.
>
> **This sequence is not typical and is not offered as evidence.** It was chosen from the
> 14 participants for whom all three policies were feasible at FAR 0.05 precisely because
> the three differ visibly, and the participant it belongs to is the single most unstable
> of the 30 in Figure 3. It illustrates the mechanism; Figure 3 shows how rare this
> participant is.

## Style

All figures: matplotlib only, no other plotting library; 300 dpi PNG plus vector PDF with
TrueType fonts embedded (`pdf.fonttype 42`); the Okabe–Ito colour-blind-safe palette, with
the three-hue categorical subset validated by the palette validator (all six checks pass;
worst adjacent CVD ΔE 11.0 under deuteranopia). Sequential encodings are single-hue,
light to dark — never a rainbow ramp, which would imply category boundaries the data does
not have. No titles inside the images. Axis labels carry units. Every figure fits a
single column of about 8.5 cm except Figure 1, which is 8.8 cm wide and tall. Each script
measures its own artists against the canvas before saving and aborts if any label would be
truncated, because `bbox_inches='tight'` crops rather than extends and would otherwise cut
labels silently.
