# Document 03 — Methodological Evaluation Framework

**Date:** 17 September 2026
**Status:** Independent audit document; a research-design specification, not an implementation record. Does not constitute the final research paper.

## Purpose

To specify — and cross-validate against independently verified external methodological literature — the research design needed to compare multiple decision-layer stabilization mechanisms on security, temporal stability, and responsiveness under matched operating conditions. This document does not report any executed experiment; it evaluates whether the design already present in this project's own scaffolding files (`01_preregistration_template.md`, `02_splice_benchmark_spec.md`, `03_mechanism_reference.md`, `04_experiment_plan.md` — all confirmed present in the project repository, Level-1 evidence) is methodologically sound, and where external literature supports, refines, or challenges it.

## Methodology / scope

I treat the four existing scaffolding files as **primary project evidence** (Level 1 — they are uploaded project artifacts, unlike the "Stage 1 audit" referenced in the task brief, which I could not locate — see Document 01 and Document 04). I independently verified four methodological literatures against this existing design: (1) matched-operating-point and DET-curve reporting for biometric verification, (2) robustness of ROC/frequency-count reporting versus single-threshold metrics, (3) open-set biometric evaluation protocol design, and (4) correct statistical procedure for comparing multiple classifiers/mechanisms across repeated (per-user) measurements, plus one empirical result on preprocessing-order bias relevant to the data-splitting design.

## Evidence

### A. Matched-operating-point comparison methodology

[Ahmed & Imtiaz, "Beyond ROC-AUC: Operating-Point Performance Reporting for Biometric Verification," arXiv:2606.20680 (June 2026), Clarkson University](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20680) demonstrates that full-AUC/EER-based rankings between two face-verification models (ArcFace vs. FaceNet) can **reverse** when compared at a matched, application-relevant false-match-rate operating point instead, using DET curves and bootstrap paired significance testing to establish which differences are statistically robust rather than a single-run artifact.

- **Alignment with existing project design:** the existing `03_mechanism_reference.md` "Matched-operating-point procedure" (select the grid cell whose ANGA-time matches a pre-registered target, freeze it, report on held-out test sequences, and additionally plot the full grid as a frontier curve) is directly consistent with this external methodology. The plan to report the full ANGA-vs-ANIA frontier, not just the matched point, mirrors Ahmed & Imtiaz's DET-curve recommendation.
- **Gap:** Ahmed & Imtiaz's bootstrap paired significance testing is at the level of individual score pairs across models; the existing plan's `04_experiment_plan.md` specifies "bootstrap 95% CIs on curve positions (resample users)." Resampling **users** (not individual frames or sequences) is the methodologically correct unit for this design, because frames within a user are not independent — this should be stated explicitly and justified in the eventual paper, citing the general bootstrap-for-dependent-data principle, since Ahmed & Imtiaz's own resampling unit (independent verification pairs) does not directly transfer to the temporally correlated, per-user sequence data used here.

### B. Robustness of frequency-count/ROC reporting versus single-threshold metrics

[Sugrim, Liu, McLean & Lindqvist, "Robust Performance Metrics for Authentication Systems," NDSS 2019](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/ndss2019_06A-3_Sugrim_slides.pdf) show empirically, using a keystroke-dynamics dataset (a CA-adjacent modality), that two systems with near-identical EER (0.185 vs. 0.198) have substantially different TPR at a matched FPR = 0.1 (0.60 vs. 0.776) — demonstrating that single-threshold aggregate metrics like accuracy or EER can obscure operationally important differences that only appear when comparing at a fixed, matched point.

- **Alignment:** this is direct, close-to-domain support for the existing plan's decision to report ANGA-time (matched genuine-lockout rate) as the fixed comparison point rather than an aggregate metric like overall accuracy, and to plot the full frontier rather than a single number.
- **Extension:** Sugrim et al. also recommend reporting the full Frequency Count of Scores (score distribution), not just ROC — the existing plan does not currently specify reporting the raw calibrated-probability distribution per mechanism/user. Adding this as a supplementary diagnostic (not a primary metric) would strengthen the eventual paper's ability to explain *why* one mechanism's frontier differs from another's, beyond the summary statistics.

### C. Open-set biometric evaluation protocol design

[Su, Kim, Liu, Jain & Liu, "Open-Set Biometrics: Beyond Good Closed-Set Models," arXiv:2407.16133](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16133v1) formalizes the mated/non-mated probe distinction and FNIR@FPIR (false-negative identification rate at a fixed false-positive identification rate) as the standard for open-set biometric evaluation, explicitly warning that closed-set-style accuracy metrics do not transfer to the open-set case where impostors may be entirely unseen at training time.

- **Alignment:** the existing `02_splice_benchmark_spec.md` design (held-out impostor users I(g) never used in g's training; test-period-only impostor blocks) is structurally an open-set protocol in Su et al.'s sense — genuine users are "mated," impostors are "non-mated" and unseen. This is a methodologically correct choice already made in the existing scaffolding, and it can be explicitly framed and cited as consistent with current open-set biometric evaluation standards in the eventual paper, strengthening its methodological positioning.
- **Terminology bridge to flag:** the existing files use ANGA-time/ANIA-time (time-normalized, continuous-authentication-specific metrics attributed to Bours et al. 2015 per the project knowledge wiki) rather than FNIR@FPIR (Su et al.'s point-based open-set metric family). These are not interchangeable — ANGA/ANIA are temporal/duration-based, FNIR@FPIR are event-rate-based. The eventual paper should explain this relationship explicitly (e.g., a false lock during a genuine segment is analogous to a false-positive identification event, and ANGA-time is the mean time between such events) rather than treating them as the same thing or citing Su et al. as though it directly validates ANGA/ANIA — it validates the *open-set framing*, not the specific metric choice.

### D. Statistical comparison of multiple mechanisms across repeated measurements

[Demšar, "Statistical Comparisons of Classifiers over Multiple Data Sets," JMLR 7 (2006)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v7/demsar06a.html) establishes: paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test for comparing **two** classifiers across multiple datasets/trials; for comparing **more than two** classifiers, an omnibus Friedman test first, followed by a Nemenyi or Bonferroni-Dunn post-hoc test if the Friedman test is significant — rather than running all pairwise tests directly, which inflates Type-I error even after correction, and rather than treating a parametric ANOVA/paired-t assumption as safe by default given typically non-normal, small-sample performance distributions.

- **Alignment:** the existing plan's **primary** comparison (hysteresis vs. the single best validation-selected smoother, paired Wilcoxon, n = 60 users) matches Demšar's two-classifier recommendation exactly, and correctly restricts the primary hypothesis test to one comparison to control multiplicity, consistent with `01_preregistration_template.md` §3's "ONE, to control multiplicity" framing.
- **Refinement recommended:** the plan's **secondary** comparisons ("all others, Holm-corrected" — implying pairwise Wilcoxon tests across the remaining 6–8 mechanisms, Holm-adjusted) is a valid, defensible approach, but Demšar's recommended alternative — a single Friedman test across all mechanisms as an omnibus check that "mechanism matters at all," followed by Nemenyi/Bonferroni-Dunn post-hoc comparisons only if significant — is the field-standard procedure specifically designed for this exact scenario (>2 classifiers, same set of "datasets" i.e. users) and is somewhat more standard in the ML/pattern-recognition literature than ad hoc Holm-corrected pairwise Wilcoxon. **This should be added as the primary secondary-analysis procedure, with the existing Holm-corrected pairwise approach retained as a supplementary sensitivity check**, since it is already specified and not wrong, merely less standard.
- **Sample-size caution repeated:** Demšar explicitly frames the unit of comparison as "datasets" (here: users, n = 60 minus exclusions). `04_experiment_plan.md`'s "Statistical minimums" section already states this correctly ("n = 60 users... for paired tests"). This is an important existing safeguard against a common error — treating each *frame* or each *sequence* as an independent statistical unit, which would badly inflate apparent significance since frames within a user's sequence are highly autocorrelated. This should remain explicit and prominent in the final methods section.

### E. Preprocessing-order bias (SMOTE and cross-validation)

[Demircioğlu, "Bias in Machine Learning Studies with Oversampling and Simplified Cross-Validation," *Scientific Reports* 14:11563 (2024), DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-62585-z](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-62585-z) provides empirical evidence, across 15 datasets, that applying oversampling (e.g., SMOTE) **before** cross-validation splitting (rather than inside each training fold only) produces measurably inflated, biased performance estimates.

- **Alignment:** the existing `04_experiment_plan.md` Exp 1 detail already specifies the correct order — "Pipeline fitted inside training fold only: median imputation → RF Gini selection (top k) → SMOTE → GB → calibration on validation fold" — meaning SMOTE is applied strictly after the train/test split and only within the training fold. This is the leakage-safe order Demircioğlu's results support.
- **Direct relevance to the reported Stage 1 facts:** the task brief's user-reported "Stage 1 audit" facts (Document 01/04) describe the *original* pre-pivot experiment as using "a random row-level 80/20 split" and "SMOTE" without specifying fold discipline. If the original experiment applied SMOTE before splitting, or split at the row level rather than chronologically/by-subject (which independently risks temporal leakage regardless of SMOTE), Demircioğlu (2024) is now available as direct, citable, external empirical evidence for *why* that would produce an inflated, non-generalizable result — reinforcing (not just asserting) the methodological concern already flagged in Document 01 of the earlier Stage 1 audit. This should be cited explicitly in Document 04 when discussing the original experiment's limitations, since it upgrades a project-internal concern to one with independent empirical backing.

## Conclusions

**Evidence:** The four existing project scaffolding files already specify a matched-operating-point, open-set, subject-independent, fixed-score-generator comparative design that aligns closely with four independently verified pieces of current (2019–2026) methodological literature on biometric/authentication evaluation. No external source was found that identifies a design flaw requiring a structural rebuild.

**Interpretation:** This design, as specified, is methodologically capable of testing RQ1/RQ2/RQ4 as stated in `01_preregistration_template.md`. The one genuine refinement identified — using a Friedman+Nemenyi/Bonferroni-Dunn omnibus test as the primary secondary-analysis procedure (with the existing Holm-corrected pairwise approach kept as a supplementary check) — is a strengthening of statistical rigor, not a correction of an error. The design's own original contributions (the context-matching Condition M construction, the margin/dwell component ablation) have no located external precedent and should be presented as project-original methodological choices in the eventual paper, not attributed to any source.

**Recommendation:** Adopt the Friedman+post-hoc procedure as the primary multi-mechanism secondary analysis; keep the existing bootstrap-CI-on-resampled-users approach for frontier-curve uncertainty (correctly resampling at the user level, not the frame or sequence level); add raw calibrated-score-distribution reporting as a supplementary diagnostic per Sugrim et al. (2019); and, when writing Document 04 and the eventual methods chapter, cite Demircioğlu (2024) explicitly as external empirical evidence for why the *original* (pre-pivot) experiment's reported row-level split and unspecified SMOTE fold discipline are methodologically risky — while continuing to describe the *new* pipeline's existing training-fold-only SMOTE order as already correct per this same evidence.
